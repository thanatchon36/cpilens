\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Classification is the step that turns a list of products into a price index, and its error
can be stated in the unit of the index. For Thai product text, transfer from other languages
gets an embedding classifier to \AccWEfiveMLPZeroShot\% of the evaluated CPI weight without a
single Thai label; official labels raise this to \AccWEfiveMLP\%; and a frontier LLM reaches
\AccWOpus\%, at a cost that a cascade cuts to \CostPctCasEfiveMLPOpus\% on the held-out list with no loss
the reference set can detect. Passed
through a simulator of the Thai CPI, the LLM's errors would misstate headline year-on-year
inflation by \BpYoyRmseHeadlineOpus{} basis points and the cheaper classifiers' by
\BpYoyRmseHeadlineBgeMLP{} to \BpYoyRmseHeadlineEfiveMLP. Accuracy does not rank methods by that error, and neither does the
error alone; both are needed, and the decomposition shows which confusions to fix. Most of
the LLMs' remaining errors are not errors of language but gaps in the documentation of the
official basket. The open questions are on the side of the data: how these numbers change
on shop text rather than the office's own, and on a real stream of products rather than an
assumed one. A labelled, probability-sampled set of web products would answer both.
