\section{Discussion: what this means for central banks in Asia}\label{sec:discussion}

Many central banks and statistical offices in Asia are building price measures from web and
scanner data in languages for which little labelled product text exists. The Thai results
suggest five points for that work. Each holds for one index, one language and the office's
own text, and should be checked on other data before it is relied on.

\paragraph{Basis points are the measure that matters.} Weighted accuracy reversed the ranking that
plain accuracy gave, and even weighted accuracy did not rank methods by their effect on
measured inflation, and basis points alone flatter a classifier that measures little of the
weight. Two classifiers that each get about \AccWEfiveMLP\% of the weight right
differ several-fold in basis points. The simulator of Section~\ref{sec:sim} needs only a
confusion matrix on held-out labels, the published weights and the published item indices.
An office that already publishes an index can therefore state the
classification error of a new source in the unit its users care about before the source
enters production. The decomposition says which confusions to fix first.

\paragraph{A published specification list is a public good.} The most useful data in this
paper are the Ministry of Commerce's price-collection specifications: Thai descriptions with
official item codes, released as open data. They served as training labels, as development
data and as the reference set, and without them no number in Section~\ref{sec:results}
could have been computed. Where an office publishes such a list, every researcher and vendor
has a common benchmark in the local language at no cost to the office.

\paragraph{LLM classification is affordable at the scale of a price index.} \NameHaiku{}
classified official Thai text with \AccWHaiku\% weighted accuracy at USD \CostHaiku{} per
million products, and the \NameEfive{}--\NameOpus{} cascade matched \NameOpus{} at USD
\CostCasEfiveMLPOpus{} per million. The whole set of LLM runs for this paper cost USD
\LLMSpendUSD. The question is then one of governance more than budget: closed models
change, responses must be cached and versioned, and an office may prefer an open model it
can run itself. The cheap methods were not close enough to make that choice free: for every cheap
method trained on all labels, the upper end of the interval for the headline error was above
the LLMs'.

\paragraph{Documentation of the basket may matter as much as the model.} The LLMs' largest
errors came from two items whose official contents differ from what their headings suggest:
TPSO's personal items include wristwatches, school bags and hair dryers, and its milk and
dairy products include margarine. A classifier can only follow the definition it is given.
A description of what each item includes and excludes, maintained with the classification,
might remove most of these errors for the LLMs; this paper does not test that, and whether
such a description can be published is a decision for each office.

\paragraph{Transfer gets a long way, but not all the way.} With no Thai label at all,
non-Thai products taught an embedding classifier to place \AccWEfiveMLPZeroShot\% of the
evaluated weight, and the alignment of Thai products with their non-Thai neighbours varied
widely by item. For languages with even fewer resources than Thai, this is a usable
starting point and a guide to where official labels are needed first: in the items where
local products look least like foreign ones. But the error of the zero-shot classifier in
basis points was large, and an office would need at least a held-out labelled sample in
its own language to know how large before it publishes anything built on it.

Two cautions apply to all five points. The reference text here is the office's own short
descriptions; shop text is longer and noisier, and accuracy on it will be lower. And the
basis-point figures assume a product universe; a real stream of web products would have its
own mix. A labelled sample of the text to be classified, drawn with known probabilities,
would resolve both and is the natural next step for any office that applies these methods.
