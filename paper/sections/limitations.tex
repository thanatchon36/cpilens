\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The reference text is the office's, not the shop's.} The reference labels come
from TPSO's price-collection specifications: short, regular descriptions written by the
office for its own collection. Shop and database texts are longer, noisier and more varied.
Accuracy on the reference set is therefore accuracy on the office's vocabulary; a classifier
deployed on web or scanner data would face harder text, and the learning curve measures the
value of official labels, not of labelled shop data. The specifications are public and may
have been seen by the LLMs in pre-training.

\paragraph{Two items cannot be evaluated.} Ready-to-eat food, the heaviest in-scope item,
and sugar and confectionery have no specifications in the published file. Their
classification error is unknown, the simulator holds them at official values, and the
basis-point figures cover \RefWeightEqual\% of the headline weight rather than
\InScopeWeightEqual\%. Items with few specifications have wide intervals.

\paragraph{No human label ceiling.} The original design included a sample of Thai products
labelled by hand with a blind second pass, to measure how consistent a labeller is and to
set that against the human error rate Spectrum reports
\citep[p.~15]{bis2026spectrum}. No such labels were collected, so this paper does not
measure the label ceiling and takes the official labels as correct. Official codes can be
wrong or ambiguous too; a method that disagrees with the office is occasionally right.

\paragraph{The label descriptions are ours.} Every classifier sees the labels only through
names and short descriptions that we wrote from TPSO's item headings, and the LLMs see
nothing else. Most of the LLMs' errors come from two places where those descriptions
disagree with the office's practice: personal items that are not personal-care goods, and
margarine. We left the descriptions unchanged, since changing them after seeing the
reference set would tune on it, so the LLM scores are a lower bound on what a correct
description of the official item contents would give.

\paragraph{The development data are not the deployment text.} Cascade thresholds were chosen
on the Thai weak-label validation split, which is Open Food Facts text. The confidence of the
cheap classifiers on that text did not carry over to official specifications, and most
cascades routed too few products. An office would have to choose thresholds on data like the
text it classifies.

\paragraph{The product universe is assumed.} The reference set is a list, not a stream of
products, so the shares $n_k$ in the simulator are assumptions. Section~\ref{sec:sim}
reports two: products in proportion to CPI weights, and in proportion to the office's list.
A real web or scanner stream has its own mix, and the error in basis points scales with it;
the hand-labelled product sample of the original design would have measured it.

\paragraph{Item weights below the published cells are estimated.} TPSO publishes weights for
its aggregates only. The equal split and the NNLS split are two transparent estimates; they
agree on the cells but not on the items inside them. Results that depend on which item
inside a cell carries the weight are reported under both.

\paragraph{The simulator measures classification error only.} It takes official item
relatives as the true price movements of every product and changes nothing but the
assignment of products to items. A real web-price index would add sampling, matching,
quality-adjustment and outlier error on top. Three modelling choices are assumptions rather
than estimates: leaked out-of-scope products move with the official non-food index, an item
that receives no products takes its group's measured movement, and the product-level
dispersion of the noise variants is assumed, not measured.

\paragraph{Closed models change.} The LLMs are reached through a commercial API. Model
revisions, prices and refusal behaviour can change after the run date; every response is
cached so that the numbers here can be reproduced, but not necessarily re-obtained. Local
compute is costed at an assumed price per GPU-hour.

\paragraph{Not comparable to Spectrum.} Spectrum classifies euro-area web data into the
ECOICOP 2018 subclasses \citep{bis2026spectrum}. Our taxonomy, language, product universe,
label source and metrics differ, so our numbers are not directly comparable with theirs,
and we do not compare them.
