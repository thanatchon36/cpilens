\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Online prices.} Prices collected from retailers' websites have become a
measurement source in their own right \citep{cavallo2016bpp}. For large multi-channel
retailers, online and offline prices are largely the same \citep{cavallo2017online}, and
online prices help forecast and nowcast inflation \citep{aparicio2020forecasting,
macias2023nowcasting}. Every such index needs each product assigned to a category of the
official basket first; this paper is about that step.

\paragraph{Classification in official statistics.} Eurostat's guides on scanner data and
web scraping for the HICP treat classification as one of the main processing steps
\citep{eurostat2017scanner, eurostat2020webscraping}, and euro-area statistics have moved
towards these sources \citep{eiglsperger2019hicp}. National statistical offices have
classified products with machine learning: the ONS with clustering and decision trees
\citep{metcalfe2016clip} and with semi-supervised label propagation into the items of the
UK CPIH basket \citep{martindale2020semi}, Statistics Netherlands with supervised classifiers, of which
logistic regression performed best \citep{harms2019cbs}, and the US Census Bureau with
text classification of product descriptions for the Economic Census
\citep{roberson2021applying}. The Australian Bureau of Statistics reported that it was
investigating machine learning to automate the classification of web-scraped products
\citep{abs2020webscraping}, and a UNECE project surveyed such uses across offices
\citep{unece2021ml}. These studies report accuracy; they do not translate it into the
error of the resulting index.

\paragraph{Project Spectrum.} The BIS Innovation Hub's Project Spectrum
\citep{bis2026spectrum} classifies the ECB's Daily Price Dataset into ECOICOP 2018
subclasses. It finds that text embeddings with classic classifiers reach accuracy comparable
to LLM prompting at a small fraction of the cost and time \citep[p.~3]{bis2026spectrum}, and
reports accuracy weighted by CPI expenditure at the subclass level
\citep[pp.~22--24]{bis2026spectrum}. It argues that, for nowcasting, errors at the subclass
level usually matter less than errors at higher levels of the hierarchy, depending on how
differently the confused categories' prices move \citep[p.~25]{bis2026spectrum}, and it names
two next steps: testing the approach on other data sets and languages, and building CPI
indices from the classified data to back-test them against official inflation
\citep[p.~30]{bis2026spectrum}. This paper takes up the first for one non-European language and
complements the second: it does not back-test a web-price index, but measures the error a
given confusion matrix adds to measured inflation, in basis points. Our setting differs from Spectrum's in taxonomy, language,
product universe and labels, so the numbers are not directly comparable.

\paragraph{Models.} We use multilingual sentence encoders \citep{reimers2019sbert,
wang2024me5, chen2024bgem3} and a fine-tuned multilingual encoder \citep{conneau2020xlmr}.
LLMs have been shown to match or beat crowd workers on text annotation
\citep{gilardi2023chatgpt}, and cascades that send only hard items to an expensive model can
cut cost at little loss of accuracy \citep{chen2023frugalgpt}. Calibrated confidence
\citep{guo2017calibration} makes selective classification possible, trading coverage for
accuracy \citep{geifman2017selective}; we weight both axes of that trade-off by CPI
expenditure.
